What Is Incident Response?

What Is Incident Response?

Incident response is the structured process used by a security team to detect, investigate, contain, and recover from a security incident.

The goal is to understand what happened, limit the impact, remove the cause when possible, and restore normal operations.

Why Is Incident Response Important?

Security incidents can affect accounts, devices, applications, networks, and sensitive information. A clear response process helps security teams handle incidents in an organized way instead of reacting without a defined process.

The Main Stages of Incident Response

A commonly used incident response process can be summarized as:

Preparation → Detection → Analysis → Containment → Eradication → Recovery → Lessons Learned

Preparation

Preparation happens before an incident occurs. Security teams establish procedures, monitoring, logging, access controls, communication methods, and tools that may be needed during an investigation.

Detection and Analysis

The process begins when suspicious activity is identified through an alert, security tool, user report, or other source.

Analysts then investigate the available evidence, including logs, network activity, endpoint events, authentication records, and other relevant information.

The objective is to determine what happened, which systems or accounts may be involved, and whether the activity represents a security incident.

Containment

Containment focuses on limiting the spread or impact of an incident.

Depending on the situation, this may involve isolating a device, disabling an account, blocking malicious network activity, or applying other temporary controls.

Eradication

Eradication focuses on removing the cause or remaining traces of the incident.

This may include removing malicious software, eliminating unauthorized access, changing compromised credentials, or addressing the vulnerability that allowed the activity to occur.

Recovery

Recovery involves returning affected systems and services to normal operation in a controlled way.

Security teams may monitor restored systems closely to verify that the original suspicious activity does not return.

Lessons Learned

After an incident, the team can review what happened and identify improvements.

This may include improving detection rules, updating security controls, improving logging, changing procedures, or addressing gaps discovered during the investigation.

Role of a SOC Analyst

SOC analysts are often involved in the early stages of incident response. They monitor alerts, perform triage, investigate evidence, build timelines, document findings, and escalate confirmed or suspected incidents according to organizational procedures.

The analyst may work with other teams when containment, system recovery, or deeper forensic investigation is required.

Simple Incident Response Example

Imagine a SOC receives an alert showing repeated failed logins followed by a successful login from an unusual source.

The analyst investigates the authentication logs, identifies related activity, checks the affected account and device, and builds a timeline.

If the evidence indicates unauthorized access, the response may include containing the account, investigating the affected system, reviewing additional activity, and restoring secure access.

Incident Response and Documentation

Documentation is an important part of incident response. Analysts should record relevant timestamps, affected systems, indicators, evidence, actions taken, and investigation findings.

Good documentation helps other team members understand the incident and provides useful information for future investigations.

Key Takeaway

Incident response provides a structured approach to handling security incidents from detection through recovery.

For SOC analysts, understanding the process helps connect everyday activities such as alert triage, log analysis, investigation, and escalation to the larger incident response lifecycle.
